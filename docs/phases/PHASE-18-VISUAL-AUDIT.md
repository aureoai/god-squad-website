# PHASE 18 — VISUAL AUDIT

**GOD SQUAD — Shopify Online Store 2.0 theme**  
**Date:** 2026-09-25  
**Scope:** final polish and award-level UI/UX QA. Not a redesign.

---

## How this audit was produced

Two independent passes, deliberately different in kind.

**Pass 1 — measured.** Harnesses render the real `.liquid` files against mock
Shopify data and read computed styles out of a browser. This is what produced the
design-coherence table, the leading measurements, the chevron angles and the
spacing figures quoted below. A measurement is quoted only where one was taken.

**Pass 2 — reviewed.** Fourteen agents across seven dimensions (typography,
spacing, buttons, icons, legacy code, motion and states, copy and brand). Each
dimension was audited by one agent and every finding adversarially verified by a
second. They returned **98 confirmed findings**, which deduplicate to **70 distinct
defects** — one defect can arrive four times wearing four labels, and the chevron
below arrived seven times.

**Every finding in this table was then re-checked by hand before any action was
taken.** That mattered: two well-argued, "CONFIRMED" findings did not survive
contact with the specification, and are marked REJECTED with the citation.

### What this audit could NOT assess

There is **no merchant photography in this project.** Phase 3 recorded sourcing —
not processing — as the ceiling, and no merchant photography exists. The harness
renders six placeholder images copied from the prototype's own folder, which are
AI-generated with unconfirmed rights — approximate imagery, not the real thing.
The theme itself ships zero image files.
Image cropping, focal points, image quality, art direction and the visual rhythm
of real photography against real copy are the core of a visual audit, and none of
them is assessable here. Everything below audits the *reservation* — the box the
picture goes in — never the picture. Any claim to have reviewed the imagery would
be invented.

## The two findings that were rejected, and why

Both were returned CONFIRMED by a verifier with evidence attached. Both are wrong,
and both would have made the theme worse.

**1. "Three disclosure chevrons render at two sizes — 24px on the product page,
16px in the cart note and filter groups."** Rated HIGH. The sizes do differ. But
`PHASE-2-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` line 1428 assigns the chevron *both*: "`--icon-sm` 16px
inline; `--icon-md` in controls". A 24px glyph beside a 12px filter label would be
the defect. **Only the direction half of this finding was acted on.**

**2. "'Add to bag' is the only 'bag' string in a purchase flow whose every other
string says 'cart'."** Rated HIGH, and factually correct — 14 "cart" strings
against it. But it is a *decided* term, not drift: `PHASE-2` line 943 writes
"add-to-bag is a `<button>` even though the prototype's CTAs are anchors", and
`PHASE-12` line 209 reads 'the enabled "Add to bag" button already says it'. The
action is "add to bag" and the container is the "cart" by choice. Unifying the
brand voice is **your** call, not a silent fix. See Decisions Required.
